# ATLAS — FY2025 Research Findings

## 1. Overview

The primary analysis examined 18 Spearman rank correlations between
six corporate governance variables and three financial performance
outcomes across ten companies.

The results are exploratory associations, not causal estimates.

### Selected observations

- Board independence had a positive association with net profit
  margin (rho = 0.523).
- Board size had a negative association with revenue growth
  (rho = -0.729).
- Independent committee membership had a positive association
  with net profit margin (rho = 0.609).
- CEO incentive compensation had positive associations with net
  profit margin (rho = 0.539) and revenue growth (rho = 0.612).
- CEO incentive compensation had a relatively weak association
  with operating cash flow margin (rho = 0.115).

These coefficients describe the sample's rank patterns. They do not
show that changes in governance caused changes in performance.

## 4. Leave-One-Out Sensitivity

Four selected relationships were recalculated after omitting each
company individually.

| Governance variable              | Financial outcome   |   Full-sample rho |   LOO minimum |   LOO maximum | Direction consistent   |
|:---------------------------------|:--------------------|------------------:|--------------:|--------------:|:-----------------------|
| Board size                       | Revenue growth      |            -0.729 |        -0.822 |        -0.656 | True                   |
| CEO incentive compensation       | Net profit margin   |             0.539 |         0.367 |         0.767 | True                   |
| Independent committee membership | Net profit margin   |             0.609 |         0.411 |         0.725 | True                   |
| Board independence               | Net profit margin   |             0.523 |         0.343 |         0.650 | True                   |

The selected relationships between board size and revenue growth,
CEO incentive compensation and net profit margin, independent
committee membership and net profit margin, and board independence
and net profit margin retained their original direction in every
leave-one-out calculation.

The CEO incentive compensation/operating cash flow margin
relationship was not directionally consistent in the broader
robustness analysis: its leave-one-out coefficient range crossed
zero. It should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

## 5. Within-Industry Comparisons

The within-industry analysis compares the direction of differences
between the two companies in each of the five industries.

For CEO incentive compensation:

- Net profit margin: 4 same-direction pairs and 1 opposite-direction pair.
- Revenue growth: 2 same-direction pairs and 3 opposite-direction pairs.
- Operating cash flow margin: 1 same-direction pair and 4 opposite-direction pairs.

There are only five industry pairs, so these comparisons are descriptive
and should not be treated as inferential statistical evidence.

## 6. Interpretation

The results suggest that some governance-performance associations
are more stable than others within this particular sample.

The board-size/revenue-growth, CEO incentive compensation/net-profit-
margin, independent committee membership/net-profit-margin, and
board independence/net-profit-margin relationships retained their
directions in the selected leave-one-out checks.

The CEO incentive compensation/operating cash flow margin relationship
was less stable.

The results also demonstrate why industry sensitivity checks matter:
excluding banks changed several coefficients and their magnitudes.

## 7. Important Qualifications

- The sample includes only ten purposively selected companies.
- Each industry contains only two companies.
- Governance observations are from FY2025.
- Correlation does not establish causation.
- Financial and governance measurements are not perfectly comparable
  across every company.
- The results should not be generalized to all publicly traded firms.
